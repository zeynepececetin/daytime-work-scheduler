The need

A monthly puzzle solved by hand

Every month one specialist assigned about 27 anesthesiologists to 25 work areas for every working day — by hand.

  • Each area carries a different workload score (200 to 5,000 points), and doctors expect their monthly totals to be equal.
  • When someone falls ill or a room closes mid-month, everything has to be reworked — and reported to the hospital administration.
  • The schedule must respect many interlocking rules: the day off after a night shift (different for Friday and weekend shifts), leave, 1–2 clinic and endoscopy days per person in different units, rooms a doctor is exempt from, at most two days in the same room, which rooms may be combined when doctors are short…

Goal: a program that never breaks a rule, makes the monthly totals as equal as possible, and fits the department's real workflow.

The schedule

A full month in seconds — and every hard rule kept

Room × day view of the generated month. Colours mark clinics and endoscopy; an asterisk marks a doctor covering two rooms that day. Status pills summarise rules and requests.

Under the hood: an optimisation algorithm (simulated annealing) explores millions of possible assignments.

  • 15 hard rules are never broken — e.g. no work the day after a night shift; a room with a location constraint can only be paired with specific rooms.
  • Preferences are met where possible, in a fixed priority order.
  • Fairness: monthly totals are pulled as close as possible to each doctor's fair target (reduced pro rata for leave).
  • Month to month: doctors rotate through rooms they did not work last month, and clinic/endoscopy days alternate between 1 and 2.

Real life happens

Mid-month changes without starting over

A doctor reports sick: affected cells turn dark red, past days stay locked, and the program proposes fixes — from “fewest changes” to “most balanced” — showing how many cells change and how fair each option is.
Each finalised revision produces an official change report for the administration: date, room, previous doctor, new doctor, reason.

Control stays with people

Original, revised and draft versions

The algorithm's original schedule is never overwritten. Manual edits happen in a draft with undo/redo; the program never blocks the scheduler, but reports any rule broken and every change in points. Finalised drafts become numbered revisions with a full history.

Outputs

  • Official list in the administration's Excel template
  • Daily schedule and per-doctor schedule (PDF / image)
  • Change report for every revision (PDF)
  • Personal schedule for each doctor (PDF / Excel)

Privacy

  • Doctors see only published schedules; access is by invitation only.
  • The desktop version keeps its data in an encrypted file.
